AI Startup Landscape — 653 Companies, May 2026

Methodology and findings from a thesis-driven scan of AI/data startups at seed–Series B

Market Analysis
AI Startups
Decision Systems
Published

May 8, 2026

The question

The AI startup landscape in May 2026 contained roughly 5,000–10,000 companies globally at seed–Series B claiming some AI/data wedge. Standard job-search advice — “apply broadly, see what sticks” — doesn’t survive a universe where most companies are too early to sponsor a visa, too engineering-only to need a senior data operator, or too AI-veneer to last 24 months.

The question I needed an answer to: out of the addressable universe of vertical-AI / data-infrastructure / forward-deployed-AI startups, which ~10 are worth a serious application and interview cycle?

What I did

Built and ran a multi-agent screening pipeline (see Startup Screener Pipeline) across the AI/data startup universe at seed-Series B. The pipeline:

  • Surveyed 653 unique companies across 14 verticals (insurance, healthcare, fintech back-office, legal, PropTech, climate, HR, GTM, data infra, AI evals, stealth, wildcards)
  • Scored each on 12 dimensions weighted toward problem quality, defensibility, founder-market fit, immigration feasibility, and fit for a senior data/AI/operator profile
  • Deep-researched the top 20 with 18-section case studies
  • Verified visa evidence for the top 10 via primary-source LCA databases
  • Identified 3 actionable tiers: apply directly (5), find warm intro (3), monitor (2)

Sources: AlleyWatch funding announcements, TechCrunch, BusinessWire, Crunchbase, YC company directory, Tier-1 VC portfolio pages, Wellfound, niche industry press (Modern Healthcare, Insurance Journal, FinTech Futures, LegalTech News), MyVisaJobs, h1bgrader, h1bdata, USCIS E-Verify employer search, Glassdoor, LinkedIn.

Key findings

1. The senior-IC visible job-title taxonomy at AI startups is converging on “Forward-Deployed Engineer,” “AI Strategist,” and “Deployed Intelligence” — not “Data Scientist”

At most vertical-AI startups (Palantir-DNA companies especially), the senior-IC role for someone with a 10-year analytics background is not titled “Data Scientist.” That title often gets junior-coded or sits behind a PhD bar. The senior seats that map cleanly to an FS-analytics / decision-science profile carry titles like Forward Deployed Engineer, AI Strategist, Deployed Intelligence Strategist, or Solutions Architect.

Implication: Searching for “Senior Data Scientist” openings will systematically miss the best-fit seats.

2. PERM completion rate matters more than LCA volume

The conventional metric for “does this company sponsor visas?” is LCA filing count. I found this metric is misleading. Several companies in the verified top 10 had 10+ LCAs filed but 0 completed PERM applications — meaning sponsored employees consistently left before the green card process completed. One mature retailer outside the AI cohort had filed 11 PERM applications and only 1 was ever certified (the rest expired).

Implication: For a 6-7 year H-1B-to-PERM horizon, you want to verify PERM completion rate, not just LCA volume. Ask in the first recruiter call: “Has anyone in your data/analytics team completed PERM here?”

3. Insurance and healthcare AI verticals systematically over-index for senior-IC fit

Of the top 20 companies by priority, 8 operated in regulated insurance or healthcare workflow domains (claims, underwriting, prior auth, denial management, RCM). These domains punish AI-veneer plays — the customers (carriers, hospitals, RCM teams) demand evidence and audit trails. They reward decision-science discipline and risk-modeling skill that transfers cleanly from financial services.

Implication: If you have FS analytics background, vertical-AI startups in insurance/healthcare/compliance are the cleanest skill transfer — better than horizontal AI infrastructure or pure dev tooling.

4. Geography and visa cluster: NYC + Series A+ + culturally-international founder is the strongest sponsorship-feasibility combination

Among the verified visa sponsors in the screen, the modal profile was: NYC-headquartered (or with a confirmed NYC office), Series A or later, and a founding team that included at least one international-origin operator. This combination shows up disproportionately in the visa-confirmed top tier.

Implication: If visa is a constraint, focus the search on NYC + Series A+ + international-co-founder companies first. SF-only seed-stage is a worse bet on visa axis even when the company is otherwise excellent.

5. “Cursor for X” / “AI agent marketplace” plays were the most consistently downrated category

In the YC W26 batch and recent seed announcements, ~10% of companies were “Cursor for [vertical]” or horizontal AI-agent marketplaces. The screen systematically downrated these:

  • Cursor-for-X plays have a 12-24 month window before Microsoft Copilot ships the equivalent in the underlying surface
  • Horizontal agent marketplaces have unclear buyer (who pays?), unclear trust mechanism (who verifies agent quality?), and unclear repeat-usage economics
  • A higher percentage of these companies (vs. vertical-AI peers) scored above 4 on the Hype/BS-Risk dimension

Implication: Treat these categories with extra skepticism. They generate fundraising press but the unit economics often don’t survive the first refresh of the underlying foundation model.

Recommendation

For an immigrant senior data IC targeting AI/data startups in 2026:

  1. Filter aggressively on visa-confirmed companies. Don’t trust inferred-from-cap-table signals. Pull primary-source LCA + PERM data before investing interview cycles.
  2. Apply to NYC + Series A+ + culturally-international-founder targets first. This profile has the best visa odds.
  3. Target vertical-AI in regulated industries. Insurance, healthcare, compliance, RegTech, and finance back-office reward the same skills your FS analytics background built.
  4. Target Forward-Deployed Engineer / AI Strategist / Solutions Architect titles, not Data Scientist. The senior-IC compensation tier hides under these names.
  5. Negotiate PERM commitment in writing at offer stage. Companies with LCA history but no PERM precedent need an explicit commitment.

What surprised me

The biggest surprise was the gap between inferred visa friendliness and verified visa friendliness. The initial scoring pass scored ~60% of “visa-friendly” candidates correctly when checked against MyVisaJobs / h1bgrader. The rest were corrected — usually downward — by primary-source verification. The inference signal (cap table size, headcount, NYC HQ) is correlated with but not equal to actual sponsorship willingness.

Second surprise: the founders’ national-origin background was a stronger predictor of sponsorship culture than the company’s funding stage. Two well-funded SF Series B companies with all-US-citizen founders sponsored less than a smaller Series A with a foreign-born CEO.

What I’d interrogate further

  • PERM cohort outcomes. Of the H-1B holders sponsored 3+ years ago at top targets, how many are still at the company today vs. departed? This is the actual signal that matters.
  • Job-title-to-LCA mapping at scale. I’d want to systematically map every job posting to the LCA-sponsored titles by company, so I could automatically flag postings unlikely to result in a visa-sponsorable role.
  • Cross-vertical second-order effects. Several “horizontal AI infra” companies in my screen had FS customer overlap (Stripe, Brex, Plaid). The FS background creates a usable wedge there even when the company isn’t FS-vertical.

Data & code

Underlying company data and rankings are private (commercial sensitivity around individual companies and visa inferences). The methodology and scoring framework are documented in the Startup Screener Pipeline write-up.

The pipeline can be re-run monthly using the delta-template I built. As of this analysis date (May 2026), the universe had been refreshed twice — once in the initial scan and once via targeted gap-fill across underserved verticals (Legal, PropTech, HR, Climate).
