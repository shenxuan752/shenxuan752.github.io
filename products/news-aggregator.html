Daily News Aggregator with WhatsApp Push

Personalized AI ingestion, filtering, and summarization pipeline delivered as a morning digest to my phone

AI Agents
Workflow Automation
Research Pipeline
Personal-AI
Published

October 19, 2025

Type: Product · Domain: Information workflow / signal extraction Stack: Python · Anthropic Claude API · Twilio WhatsApp · RSS / source APIs · cron Status: Production-ready, running daily · Role: Builder

One-line summary

A daily content pipeline that pulls articles from 12 curated tech and investment sources, filters out clickbait (~36% drop rate), deduplicates near-identical stories across publishers, summarizes the top-scoring items with Claude, and pushes the digest to WhatsApp every morning. Cuts daily news reading from ~105 minutes to ~35 minutes.

Why I built this

Tech and investment news degraded sharply through 2024–25. Three problems:

  • The same press release shows up across four sites. Reading “Nvidia announces DGX Spark” on Ars Technica, then The Verge, then TechCrunch, then Hacker News is the modal news-browsing experience now. Most of the time is spent recognizing duplicates, not extracting signal.
  • Content-farm noise expanded faster than signal. Even quality publishers are running AI-generated rewrites and SEO bait. Browsing TechCrunch homepage for thirty minutes returns five real stories.
  • AI summarization tools work downstream of the problem. They summarize after the noise has already filled my reading queue. The leverage point is upstream: better source curation, better deduplication, then AI for signal extraction — not a chatbot that explains the noise more concisely.

I wanted a pipeline that did the upstream work — curate sources, dedupe across publishers, AI-summarize survivors, deliver to a medium I’d actually see (WhatsApp, not another email folder I never open).

What I built

A daily content pipeline (daily_digest.py) that:

  • Pulls from 12 curated sources — Ars Technica, Bloomberg Markets, Hacker News, The Verge, Wired, MIT Technology Review, MarketWatch, TechCrunch, plus others — configured in config/sources.yaml
  • Filters clickbait and low-quality content — title heuristics + content checks; ~36% drop rate in production
  • Deduplicates near-identical stories across publishers — clusters by headline + first-paragraph similarity, keeps the most credible publisher in each cluster
  • Summarizes top-scoring articles with Claude — 2-3 sentence summary + 3-5 bullet points + relevance score (75–85/100 for AI-summarized items, 50/100 baseline for headline-only items)
  • Splits into Tech vs Investment categories for scan-ability
  • Pushes the digest to WhatsApp via Twilio — formatted text message delivered to my phone every morning
  • Runs via cron so it just shows up; I don’t have to remember to launch it

How it works

  • Source ingestion: RSS feeds for sources that publish them, light HTML scraping for the ones that don’t, all configured per-source in YAML
  • Filtering: title/content heuristics catch the obvious clickbait; per-source rate limits prevent over-fetching
  • Deduplication: cluster articles by normalized headline + first-200-token similarity; pick the highest-quality publisher in each cluster
  • Scoring: a relevance score is computed from source quality, topic match (tech vs investment), and recency
  • AI summarization: top N articles get Claude calls for structured summaries
  • WhatsApp delivery via Twilio: this was the highest-friction part — the 24-hour WhatsApp Business messaging window is a real product constraint (multiple helper scripts in the repo handle account-state verification, message-window checks, opt-in flow)

My role

Sole builder. Picked the source list and iterated on it (the source list is the product more than the summarization is). Wrote the dedup + scoring logic, the Claude summarization prompts, the Twilio integration with all the WhatsApp window handling. Set up the cron job so the digest just lands every morning.

Output: from a real production run

  • Articles fetched: 33 from 12 sources
  • Filtered out (clickbait / low quality): 12 (36% noise drop)
  • Duplicates removed across sources: 11
  • Articles delivered to digest: 21 (8 AI-summarized + 13 with title/URL/score)
  • Active sources surfaced: 8 (out of 12 configured)
  • Average relevance (AI-summarized): 75–85/100
  • Operating cost: ~$1.50/month at current volume
  • Daily reading time: ~105 min before → ~35 min after (≈70 min/day saved)

Each AI-enhanced article in the digest includes:

  • Concise 2-3 sentence summary
  • 3-5 bullet-pointed key facts
  • Automatic topic detection
  • Relevance score (typically 75-85/100)
  • Source and publish date
  • Direct link to full article

What I learned

  • Curation is upstream of summarization. The first version of this pipeline focused on better summaries. The second focused on better source picking and deduplication. The second was dramatically more useful — same summarization quality, much less noise to summarize.
  • The delivery medium matters more than the content quality. A great digest in an email folder I never open is worth less than a decent digest in a WhatsApp thread I check 50 times a day. Match the artifact to where the decision actually happens.
  • WhatsApp’s 24-hour Business messaging window is a real product constraint. Handling it gracefully (verify account state, opt-in flow, fallback paths, multiple helper checks) took more code than the summarization pipeline. Twilio’s docs are accurate but the failure modes are subtle.
  • Cost-per-decision is the right metric for personal AI. $1.50/month for ~35 hours/month of attention saved is a 2,000× return at any reasonable hourly rate. Most personal-AI tools fail the cost-per-decision test; this one passes by a wide margin.
